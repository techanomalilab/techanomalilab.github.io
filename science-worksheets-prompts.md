# 100 Prompts for Generating Science Worksheets for 6-Year-Olds

## Brainstorming & Discussion: Learning Designer × Curriculum Designer

### Participants
- **Learning Designer (LD):** Focuses on pedagogy, engagement, cognitive load, multi-sensory learning, and age-appropriate instructional strategies.
- **Curriculum Designer (CD):** Focuses on scope & sequence, standards alignment (NGSS K-2), content accuracy, skill progression, and cross-curricular connections.

---

### Discussion Round 1: Developmental Considerations for 6-Year-Olds

**LD:** At age 6, children are typically in Piaget's preoperational-to-concrete-operational transition. They learn best through hands-on exploration, concrete examples, and visual/tactile engagement. Worksheets should supplement — not replace — real-world experimentation. We need to prioritize:
- **Short, focused tasks** (5–10 minutes per page)
- **Large fonts, clear visuals, ample white space**
- **Minimal reading required** — use pictures, icons, and simple 1–2 sentence instructions
- **Multi-modal engagement:** coloring, circling, drawing, cutting/pasting, tracing, matching
- **Scaffolded difficulty** — start with recognition, then sorting, then simple reasoning

**CD:** Agreed. From a curriculum perspective, 6-year-olds (typically Kindergarten–1st Grade) should cover these NGSS-aligned science domains:
1. **Life Science** — plants, animals, habitats, body parts, life cycles, needs of living things
2. **Earth & Space Science** — weather, seasons, day/night, rocks/soil, water cycle basics
3. **Physical Science** — pushes/pulls (forces), light/sound, properties of materials, magnets
4. **Engineering & Design** — simple problem-solving, building, testing

We should also weave in **science practices**: observing, comparing, sorting, predicting, recording, and communicating.

---

### Discussion Round 2: Worksheet Activity Types

**LD:** Let's define a toolkit of activity types that work well on paper for this age:

| Activity Type | Description | Engagement Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Circle / Color** | Identify correct items by circling or coloring | High (motor + cognitive) |
| **Match / Draw Lines** | Connect related items | Medium-High |
| **Sort / Categorize** | Cut-and-paste or draw items into groups | High |
| **Label / Trace** | Trace words or label diagram parts | Medium |
| **Sequence / Order** | Number or arrange steps in order | High (reasoning) |
| **Observe & Draw** | Draw what they see or predict | Very High (creative) |
| **Yes/No & True/False** | Simple binary questions with visuals | Medium |
| **Count & Compare** | Count items, compare quantities | Cross-curricular (math) |
| **Fill in the Blank** | Complete simple sentences with word bank | Medium |
| **Maze / Path** | Follow a path based on science criteria | High (fun + learning) |

**CD:** Excellent. I'd add that every worksheet should have a **"Big Idea"** or learning objective stated simply at the top for the parent/teacher, and the child should feel a sense of **accomplishment** at the end. Let's also ensure **cultural inclusivity** in illustrations and scenarios.

---

### Discussion Round 3: Thematic Coverage Plan

**CD:** To ensure breadth, let's distribute the 100 prompts across domains:

| Domain | Number of Prompts | Key Topics |
|---|---|---|
| Life Science | 30 | Plants, animals, insects, body, senses, habitats, life cycles, food chains, needs |
| Earth & Space Science | 25 | Weather, seasons, water cycle, rocks, soil, day/night, sun/moon, Earth care |
| Physical Science | 25 | Forces, magnets, light, sound, materials, states of matter, sinking/floating |
| Engineering & Design Thinking | 10 | Problem-solving, tools, building, testing, improving |
| Science Practices (Cross-cutting) | 10 | Observing, measuring, predicting, recording, comparing |

**LD:** I'd also suggest we vary the **cognitive demand** across Bloom's levels appropriate for this age:
- **Remember/Recognize** (~30%): identify, name, match
- **Understand** (~30%): sort, classify, explain in pictures
- **Apply** (~25%): predict, sequence, use knowledge in new context
- **Analyze/Create** (~15%): compare, draw conclusions, design

---

### Discussion Round 4: Inclusivity & Accessibility

**LD:** Important design principles for the actual worksheet generation:
- Use **diverse characters** in illustrations (different skin tones, abilities, genders)
- Include **real-world contexts** from various cultures (foods, animals, environments)
- Provide **visual supports** for emerging readers and ELL students
- Use **dyslexia-friendly fonts** and high-contrast colors
- Include **differentiation tips** (easier/harder versions noted)

**CD:** Each prompt should specify these elements so the generated worksheet is complete and usable by any family or classroom.

---

### Discussion Round 5: Prompt Structure Agreement

Both designers agree each prompt should specify:
1. **Topic & Domain**
2. **Learning Objective** (kid-friendly)
3. **Activity Type(s)**
4. **Key Vocabulary** (2–4 words)
5. **Visual/Illustration Requirements**
6. **Difficulty Level** (Easy / Medium / Challenging)

### LIFE SCIENCE (Prompts 1–30)

#### Plants

**Prompt 1:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Parts of a Plant**. The worksheet should include a large, clear illustration of a whole plant (roots, stem, leaves, flower) with blank label lines pointing to each part. Provide a word bank at the bottom (roots, stem, leaves, flower). Include a coloring activity where the child colors each part a specific color according to a key. Learning objective: "I can name the parts of a plant." Vocabulary: roots, stem, leaves, flower. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 2:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **What Plants Need to Grow**. Show four pictures: sunlight, water, soil, and a toy car. The child circles the three things plants need and crosses out the one plants do NOT need. Below, include a drawing box where the child draws their own plant in a sunny spot with water. Learning objective: "I can tell what a plant needs to grow." Vocabulary: sunlight, water, soil, grow. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 3:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Seeds and How They Grow**. Create a sequencing activity showing 4 scrambled pictures: a seed in soil, a sprout breaking through, a small plant with leaves, and a flowering plant. The child cuts and pastes (or numbers) the pictures in the correct order. Include a sentence at the bottom: "First, the seed goes in the ___." with a word bank. Learning objective: "I can put the steps of plant growth in order." Vocabulary: seed, sprout, grow, plant. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 4:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Trees in Different Seasons**. Show four identical tree outlines labeled Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter. The child draws and colors the correct appearance for each season (blossoms, full green leaves, orange/red leaves, bare branches or snow). Include a simple matching activity below connecting season names to weather icons. Learning objective: "I can show how trees change in each season." Vocabulary: seasons, spring, summer, fall, winter. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 5:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Fruits and Vegetables — Plant Parts We Eat**. Show 8 illustrated food items (apple, carrot, lettuce, strawberry, potato, broccoli, corn, celery). The child sorts them into two columns: "Fruits" and "Vegetables" by drawing lines or cutting and pasting. Include a fun fact box: "Did you know? A tomato is actually a fruit!" Learning objective: "I can sort fruits and vegetables." Vocabulary: fruit, vegetable, plant, eat. Difficulty: Medium.

The child matches each animal to its covering type by drawing lines: fur, feathers, scales, smooth skin, shell. Include a coloring section where the child colors the animal with the most colorful covering. Learning objective: "I can match animals to what covers their bodies." Vocabulary: fur, feathers, scales, skin. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 7:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Where Do Animals Live? (Habitats)**. Show a pond, a forest, a desert, and an ocean at the top. Below, show 8 animals (duck, deer, camel, whale, frog, owl, lizard, dolphin). The child draws a line from each animal to its habitat. Learning objective: "I can match animals to where they live." Vocabulary: habitat, pond, forest, desert, ocean. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 8:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **What Do Animals Eat?** Create three category boxes labeled "Plants Only (Herbivore)," "Meat Only (Carnivore)," and "Both (Omnivore)" with simple icons. Show 9 animals (cow, lion, bear, rabbit, eagle, pig, horse, shark, chicken). The child sorts each animal into the correct box. Include friendly illustrations. Learning objective: "I can sort animals by what they eat." Vocabulary: herbivore, carnivore, omnivore. Difficulty: Challenging.

**Prompt 9:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Baby Animals and Their Parents**. Show two columns: adult animals on the left (cat, dog, horse, hen, cow, duck) and baby animals on the right in scrambled order (kitten, puppy, foal, chick, calf, duckling). The child draws lines to match each baby to its parent. Include a "Draw Your Own" box to draw a baby animal they like. Learning objective: "I can match baby animals to their parents." Vocabulary: baby, parent, kitten, puppy, calf, chick. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 10:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **How Animals Move**. Show animals that walk, fly, swim, hop, and slither (dog, eagle, fish, frog, snake, horse, butterfly, whale). Create a chart with columns: Walk, Fly, Swim, Hop, Slither. The child places (writes or pastes) each animal name/picture in the correct column. Some animals may go in more than one column. Learning objective: "I can describe how different animals move." Vocabulary: walk, fly, swim, hop, slither. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 11:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Insect or Not an Insect?** Show 10 creatures (ant, spider, butterfly, ladybug, worm, bee, scorpion, grasshopper, snail, beetle). Provide a "rule box" showing that insects have 6 legs, 3 body parts, and antennae. The child circles only the insects. Below, the child counts and writes how many insects they found. Learning objective: "I can identify insects by their features." Vocabulary: insect, legs, antennae, body parts. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 12:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **The Life Cycle of a Butterfly**. Show a circular diagram with 4 stages scrambled: egg, caterpillar (larva), chrysalis (pupa), butterfly (adult). The child numbers them in order and colors each stage. Include a sentence completion: "A caterpillar changes into a ___." Learning objective: "I can describe the life cycle of a butterfly." Vocabulary: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 13:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Nocturnal vs. Daytime Animals**. Show a split scene: sunny daytime on the left, moonlit nighttime on the right. Below, show 8 animals (owl, rooster, bat, dog, raccoon, butterfly, fox, bee). The child cuts and pastes (or draws lines from) each animal to the correct side. Learning objective: "I can sort animals by when they are active." Vocabulary: nocturnal, daytime, active, night. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 14:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Pets and Wild Animals**. Show 10 animals (cat, tiger, goldfish, elephant, hamster, wolf, parrot, bear, dog, giraffe). The child sorts them into "Pets" and "Wild Animals" columns. Include a drawing box: "Draw your favorite pet or a wild animal you'd like to see." Learning objective: "I can tell the difference between pets and wild animals." Vocabulary: pet, wild, tame, nature. Difficulty: Easy.

#### Human Body & Senses

**Prompt 15:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **My Five Senses**. Show a large outline of a child's face/body with the five sense organs highlighted (eyes, ears, nose, tongue, hands). Next to each organ, show three small pictures. The child circles the picture that matches each sense (e.g., next to eyes: a rainbow, a drum, a flower — circle the rainbow). Learning objective: "I can match my senses to the right body part." Vocabulary: see, hear, smell, taste, touch. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 16:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **What Can I Do With My Senses?** Create a table with 5 rows (See, Hear, Smell, Taste, Touch) and a blank column for drawing. The child draws one thing they can experience with each sense. At the bottom, include a "Sense Detective" challenge: "Go on a walk. What do you hear? Draw 2 things." Learning objective: "I can use my senses to explore the world." Vocabulary: senses, explore, observe, discover. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 17:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Taking Care of My Body**. Show pictures of healthy and unhealthy habits: brushing teeth, eating candy all day, sleeping well, exercising, skipping hand washing, eating fruits/vegetables, drinking water, watching TV all day. The child circles the healthy habits in green and the unhealthy habits in red. Learning objective: "I can choose habits that keep my body healthy." Vocabulary: healthy, exercise, sleep, nutrition. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 18:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Bones and Muscles**. Show a friendly cartoon skeleton on one side and a child flexing muscles on the other. Include a simple labeling activity for 4 major bone areas (skull, ribs, spine, leg bones) with a word bank. Below, the child draws themselves doing an activity that uses muscles (running, jumping, lifting). Learning objective: "I can tell that bones and muscles help my body move." Vocabulary: bones, muscles, skeleton, move. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 19:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **How We Grow and Change**. Show a sequence of 4 images: a baby, a toddler, a 6-year-old child, and a grown-up. The child numbers them in order from youngest to oldest. Below, provide two boxes: "Things I could do as a baby" and "Things I can do NOW" — the child draws or writes examples in each. Learning objective: "I can describe how people grow and change." Vocabulary: baby, child, adult, grow. Difficulty: Easy.

#### Living vs. Non-Living

**Prompt 20:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Living and Non-Living Things**. Show 12 pictures in a grid: dog, rock, tree, car, bird, water, flower, book, fish, sun, mushroom, ball. The child colors living things green and non-living things blue. Include a checklist at the bottom: "Living things can: □ grow □ breathe □ eat □ move on their own." Learning objective: "I can sort things into living and non-living." Vocabulary: living, non-living, grow, breathe. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 21:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **What Living Things Need**. Show a Venn diagram with two overlapping circles: "What Plants Need" and "What Animals Need." Provide picture cards (water, sunlight, food, air, soil, shelter) for the child to place in the correct area. Items both need go in the overlap. Learning objective: "I can compare what plants and animals need." Vocabulary: needs, water, air, shelter, sunlight. Difficulty: Challenging.

#### Habitats & Ecosystems

**Prompt 22:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Life in a Pond**. Show a large pond scene illustration with blank spaces where creatures should be. Provide cut-out pictures of pond animals and plants (frog, fish, dragonfly, lily pad, turtle, cattail). The child pastes each in the correct place. Include 2 true/false questions: "Frogs can live in water and on land. True / False." Learning objective: "I can name living things found in a pond." Vocabulary: pond, frog, fish, lily pad. **Prompt 23:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Ocean Animals**. Show an ocean cross-section with surface, middle, and deep water zones. Provide 8 ocean animals (seagull, dolphin, jellyfish, sea turtle, whale, octopus, anglerfish, clownfish). The child draws or places each animal in the zone where it most likely lives. Include a coloring element. Learning objective: "I can describe where ocean animals live." Vocabulary: ocean, surface, deep, marine. Difficulty: Challenging.

**Prompt 24:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Animals in Winter**. Explain three strategies: hibernate, migrate, adapt (stay and grow thicker fur). Show animals: bear, goose, rabbit, bat, monarch butterfly, squirrel, deer, hummingbird. The child sorts each into: Hibernate, Migrate, or Stay & Adapt. Include a simple coloring activity. Learning objective: "I can tell how animals survive winter." Vocabulary: hibernate, migrate, adapt, winter. Difficulty: Challenging.

#### Food Chains

**Prompt 25:**

Learning objective: "I can put a food chain in order." Vocabulary: food chain, energy, eat, predator. Difficulty: Challenging.

#### Animal Classification

**Prompt 28:**

**Prompt 30:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **The Life Cycle of a Frog**. Show a circular life cycle diagram with stages scrambled: eggs (frogspawn), tadpole, tadpole with legs, froglet, adult frog. The child numbers the stages and draws arrows showing the cycle direction. Include a coloring activity and a fun fact: "Tadpoles breathe with gills like fish!" Learning objective: "I can order the stages of a frog's life cycle." Vocabulary: egg, tadpole, froglet, frog. Difficulty: Medium.

### EARTH & SPACE SCIENCE (Prompts 31–55)

#### Weather

**Prompt 31:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Types of Weather**. Show 6 weather types with icons: sunny, rainy, cloudy, snowy, windy, stormy. The child matches each weather word to its icon by drawing lines. Below, include a "Today's Weather" box where the child draws and writes today's weather. Learning objective: "I can identify and name different types of weather." Vocabulary: sunny, rainy, snowy, windy. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 32:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Weather Tracking Chart**. Create a 7-day weather chart (Monday–Sunday) with empty boxes. Provide weather symbol stickers/drawings (sun, cloud, rain, snow, wind) at the bottom. The child records each day's weather by drawing or pasting the correct symbol. Include a question: "Which weather happened the most this week?" Learning objective: "I can observe and record the weather every day." Vocabulary: observe, record, weather, chart. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 33:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **What Should I Wear?** Show 4 weather scenarios (hot sunny day, rainy day, cold snowy day, windy fall day). Next to each, show clothing options (shorts & t-shirt, raincoat & boots, winter coat & hat, light jacket). The child matches the right outfit to the right weather. Learning objective: "I can choose clothes that match the weather." Vocabulary: weather, clothing, warm, cool. Difficulty: Easy.

**Prompt 34:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Clouds and What They Tell Us**. Show three main cloud types with friendly illustrations: cumulus (fluffy, fair weather), stratus (flat, gray, drizzle), cumulonimbus (tall, dark, thunderstorm). The child matches each cloud to the weather it brings. Include a "Look Outside" activity: "Draw the clouds you see right now." Learning objective: "I can name cloud types and the weather they bring." Vocabulary: cumulus, stratus, cloud, storm. Difficulty: Medium.

**Prompt 35:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **The Water Cycle — A Water Drop's Journey**. Show a simple, colorful water cycle diagram with sun, clouds, rain, puddle/ocean, and arrows. Label the stages (evaporation, condensation, precipitation) with kid-friendly explanations. The child traces the arrows and fills in missing labels from a word bank. Learning objective: "I can trace the path water takes in the water cycle." Vocabulary: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, cycle. Difficulty: Challenging.

**Prompt 36:**
Generate a science worksheet page for 6-year-olds about **Rainbows**. Show a large rainbow outline with 7 bands. The child colors each band in the correct order (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet). Include a "Rainbow Recipe" explanation: "Sunlight + raindrops = rainbow!" Below, a true/false: "You can see a rainbow at night. True / False?" Learning objective: "I can name the colors of the rainbow in order." Vocabulary: rainbow, sunlight, rain, colors. Difficulty: Easy.
